# Privacy

exitcode0.net is a personal blog run by Tom Cocking in the United Kingdom. I'm the data controller for this site. If you have a question about your data, or want to use any of the rights below, email **[privacy@exitcode0.net](mailto:privacy@exitcode0.net)**.

## The short version

- There are no accounts, comments, newsletters or forms here. I never ask for your name or email.
- The site shows Google ads and, if you agree, measures visits with Google Analytics. Google's consent message asks you first, and you can change your answer at any time.
- The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which logs your IP address for security.
- I don't sell data, and I can't see who you are. What I see in Google Analytics is aggregated: page views, rough location by country and city, and device type.

## Your consent choices

On your first visit from the UK, the EU or Switzerland, Google's consent message asks how your data may be used. It's a Google-certified consent tool built on the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework, and it lists every advertising partner by name.

To change your mind later, use the round shield button in the bottom-left corner of any page. It reopens the same message.

If you decline, Google Analytics never loads. Google may still show ads, but they won't be personalised to you.

## Advertising: Google AdSense

The site is funded partly by Google AdSense. In Google's words, which Google asks every AdSense site to include:

- Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on a user's previous visits to this website or other websites.
- Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visit to this site and/or other sites on the internet.
- You may opt out of personalised advertising by visiting [Google's Ads settings](https://www.google.com/settings/ads). You can also opt out of some third-party vendors' use of cookies for personalised advertising at [www.aboutads.info](http://www.aboutads.info/choices/).

Other ad partners may also serve ads through Google. They're all listed under "Manage options" in the consent message, with links to their own privacy policies and opt-outs.

Ads that aren't personalised can still use cookies or similar identifiers, for things like limiting how often you see the same ad and detecting ad fraud. Google explains this in [How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

## Analytics: Google Analytics 4

Google Analytics loads only after you consent to storing information on your device and to Google as a vendor. If you decline, or never answer, the script isn't requested at all.

When it does run, it records which pages you view, how you arrived (a search engine, a link elsewhere), your rough location, and your device and browser type. I use this to see which guides people find useful. Google keeps event-level data for 2 months and user-level data for 14 months, after which it's deleted. Reports built from aggregated totals, such as monthly page views, are kept longer.

## Hosting: GitHub Pages

The site's files are served by GitHub Pages. GitHub says that when a Pages site is visited, the visitor's IP address is logged and stored for security purposes, whether or not the visitor has a GitHub account. I can't see these logs. See the [GitHub General Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).

## Embedded YouTube videos

A couple of posts embed YouTube videos. They use YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com), so YouTube doesn't store cookies on your device just because you opened the page. If you press play, YouTube and the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) take over from there.

## Buy Me a Coffee

The orange coffee-cup button on each page is a widget loaded from buymeacoffee.com, so Buy Me a Coffee receives the same basic request data as any website you load: your IP address and browser details. The widget doesn't set cookies on this site. If you click it and leave a tip, the [Buy Me a Coffee privacy policy](https://www.buymeacoffee.com/privacy-policy) applies.

## Affiliate links

Some posts contain affiliate or referral links, mostly Amazon UK and in one case Honeygain. Each of those posts carries a disclosure at the top. If you buy or sign up through one of those links, this site may earn a small commission or credit. It costs you nothing extra and it does not change what gets recommended.

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. Following an Amazon link takes you to Amazon, which sets its own cookies so it can credit the referral. Amazon's privacy notice covers anything you do there.

## What's stored in your browser

| Name | Set by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `pref-theme` | this site (local storage) | Remembers whether you chose light or dark mode |
| `menu-scroll-position` | this site (local storage) | Keeps the menu's scroll position on small screens |
| `FCCDCF` and similar | Google's consent message | Records your consent choices |
| Advertising cookies | Google and its ad partners | Ad delivery, measurement and fraud prevention, as set out above |
| `_ga`, `_ga_*` | Google Analytics | Only if you consent to analytics |

Neither of the site's own local storage items leaves your browser or identifies you.

## Legal basis

- **Consent** covers analytics and personalised advertising. You give it, or not, in Google's consent message, and you can withdraw it at any time.
- **Legitimate interests** cover GitHub's security logging, and the limited processing Google does to deliver non-personalised ads and stop fraud. Google sets out its legitimate interest purposes in the consent message, where you can object to them.

Google and GitHub are US companies and may process this data outside the UK. Their privacy policies, linked above, explain the safeguards they rely on for those transfers.

## Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of personal data held about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. You can also object to or restrict how it's used, and withdraw consent. Most data from your visit is held by Google and GitHub rather than by me. Their own tools are usually the fastest route, but email me and I'll help where I can.

If you're unhappy with how your data is handled, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at [ico.org.uk](https://ico.org.uk/).

## Changes

If anything here changes, for example a new ad partner or a new embedded service, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
